# Briefing Satie

Satie's agent knows how to make an audio scene; what it cannot know is your world. The brief is where you tell it.

**Say what the place is and how it should feel** in plain words: vantage (where the player stands), enclosure (a stone courtyard, a steel corridor, open moor), time, weather, mood, and what matters most ("the fountain is the heart of it", "tension is the point"). One paragraph beats a list.

**Give it the scene manifest.** Every object that can make sound: the mesh or node name as `id` (that exact id becomes the `at <id>` host your glue binds), its static host-world position in metres (+Y up; use the game's coordinates unchanged) or `motion: "moves"` for anything the game drives, a rough `radiusM` for anything bigger than a point, its material, a note on what it does. Ahead comes from the listener camera matrix; a default Three.js/Web Audio camera faces -Z. Satie-authored world layouts may call +Z their authored ahead: orient the host camera to that layout, or transform all hosts, geometry and listener poses together. Never flip only audio positions. Then the interactions:
- `events`: every event your game will fire (door, hit, pickup, shot, step). For `step`, list the surfaces. Each becomes an `on <event>` bank.
- `states`: the states your game switches (calm, alert, combat; indoors, outdoors). Each gates voices with `zone`.
- `signals`: the telemetry you will publish (speed, engine_load, health, crowd) with ranges. Each drives `follow` / `quiet` / `when`.
- `listener`: first_person, third_person, or fixed. Explicitly choose the listening perspective: render camera, character, vehicle, or a named object with a local offset. Camera and character positions are different valid choices; third person does not imply either one. Respect the developer's requested position and orientation policy through possession and camera changes.
- `player`: the object id the person plays as (the character; what they ride), whatever the listening position: in third person the ears may be on the camera, the player is still the character. Mark the player's own events `by: "<player id>"` (steps, jumps, the horn, doors they open), and put sounds their thing makes `at` it. An event done with whichever one of a kind of object (the horn or door of the car being driven) names the kind: `with: "car"`. The mixer shows all of these as "you".
- Labels are the names players see: a car's model ("Comet GT"), a character's name, "porch door". Never a description with the id's number ("Actual stable vehicle 0"); the mixer counts things that share a name ("Comet GT 2").

**Let the glue describe the scene; let Satie look at it.** `manifestFrom(scene)` (in the glue below) walks your named objects and returns `scene.objects` with positions, bounding boxes and materials: sized objects become occluders the engine renders through, and the ground material shapes footsteps and reflections. Add `scene.rooms` for every enclosed space you know (size, centre, floor/walls/ceiling): they become `room` declarations, and Satie puts the voices inside them `in <room>`. Take 2 to 4 screenshots from the player's vantage, keep each with satie_asset (kind "picture") and pass the returned ids as `scene.screenshots` (`{ assetId, note }`; inline PNG/JPEG base64 still works for small shots): Satie surveys enclosure, materials, distances and off-screen sources before composing. A manifest that is only a list of names gets open-air defaults; that is the shallow version.

**Ask before you spend.** A brief that never says where the player listens gets first person; one that never names the ground gets generic footsteps; one that never says what must stay audible gets nothing protected. Those defaults are reasonable and invisible, and the developer finds them only by listening. `satie_brief { brief, scene }` is free and lists them as questions, each with what Satie will assume and why it matters. Put the load-bearing ones to the developer in their own words, two or three at a time, not a form; fold each answer into the brief as one sentence ("third person, distance judged from the character"; "the bell must cut through combat"; "flagstone in the hall, gravel in the yard"). A scene composed without asking still reports what it assumed in `openQuestions`.

**Repeated objects retain independent behavior.** Name copies with stable ids and bind each. A count/class voice is suitable only when its controls and lifecycles genuinely match. Independent vehicles need per-object controls, state groups, source ids and hosts; copying a traffic pass-by recording cannot represent those cars. Reusing a steady source recording is possible with decorrelated starts, but it does not create new physical events or guarantee freedom from audible repetition.

**Expect fewer voices than objects.** A good scene is one or two beds, a handful of fixtures that truly make sound, and the events; a lantern, a bench or a parked car is silent. Beds (rain, wind, water, crowd) come from library recordings when the library has them; generation is for the specific and eventful. Every generated take is checked by a listening model at purchase; `satie_realize` names the takes it doubts in `suspectTakes` (with what was heard instead). When a voice's takes ALL come back wrong, the generator cannot make that sound (a rope halyard tick came back as seven whooshes): call `satie_compose` again with the script and a brief that quotes the failures; it replaces the voice with a sound the generator can render, a library recording, or silence. Then realize again.

**Then wire what Satie asked for.** The result names the hosts to bind and the events it expects; your glue is a handful of calls (see the engine section): bind each host, call `update(camera)` per frame, fire events where things happen, publish signals, switch states.

**What each interaction becomes, and the call your game makes.**

| Interaction | Satie writes | Your game calls |
|---|---|---|
| Discrete action (door, pickup, hit, shot, UI) | `on door …` bank with `within`, `cooldown`, `variants` | `satie.event('door', {x,y,z})` |
| Footsteps / landing on a surface | `on step … {text}`, `on land … {text}` | Authoritative foot/ground contact forwarded through HostAudioBridge, with position, material and occurrence timestamp |
| Something answering something else | `emit spark` on the cause, `on spark` on the answer | nothing; the engine chains them |
| Continuous parameter (engine load, speed, tension) | `volume follow engine_load …`, `pitch follow speed …` | `satie.signal('engine_load', 0.7)` (built-ins need no call: `distance`, `gaze`, `speed`) |
| Threshold / a look | `when gaze 0.9to1`, `when distance 0to3` | nothing; the engine reads the listener |
| Game states | `zone calm strict`, `zone alert combat strict` | `satie.state('alert')`; `satie.state(null)` |
| The chosen listening perspective | independent of audio source geometry | `audio.bindListener(camera)`, or an explicitly chosen character/vehicle with local offset and orientation object |
| A thing you drive (car, NPC, drone) | voices `at car`, `on horn` | `satie.bind('car', carMesh)`; `satie.event('horn', carPos)` |
| Heard through a wall | `occlusion 0.6` | nothing |

**Contact Foley follows actual contact.** For an animated or physical character, use resolved per-foot ground-contact edges or animation contact notifies corroborated by support geometry, with the hit material and world contact point. The reusable FootContactTracker explicitly declares physics or animation authority; it suppresses duplicate planted contacts and rearms from separation, not a blind timer. The host owns grounded/support state, animation phase and any measured normal impulse/speed. Do not infer force from travel speed, play a walking loop, or poll distance independently of the displayed gait. An explicit distance fallback exists for controllers without foot contacts; it consumes only collision-resolved self-propelled ground travel and is labelled distance-fallback. It cannot establish animation synchronization or measured contact physics. Test stopping, blocked movement, idle leg animation, jumps/landings, teleport/reset, platforms, turning, slopes and stairs at different frame rates. Six to eight distinct usable contact performances and no-repeat selection help timbre variation, but cannot fix incorrect contact timing.

**Ducking with states.** A matching `zone` plays untouched. A nonmatching state muffles a soft zone or silences a strict one; untagged voices stay unchanged. To duck beds while reading, declare normal and reading and tag the beds `zone normal`, then call `satie.state('reading', 0.7)`. Return with `state('normal')` or `state(null)`. For level-only ducking, use a declared signal with `volume follow` and have the host publish it.

**Reading the script.** Satie writes plaintext, one comment line per voice saying why it is there. Layers, quietest to loudest: `bed` (the place itself), fixtures `at` your objects, `on` events, occasional life, `zone` states. Every voice states `spl` (dB at 1 m, physics not taste); gen prompts describe the SOURCE close and dry because the engine owns acoustics. You can read all of this; you should not need to write it.

**Cost.** Composing, briefing, auditing and checking are free: Satie pays their model time. Realizing is about 1¢ per NEW take (a `variants 3` voice is 3 takes); takes already in Satie's pool are free.

The script is plaintext and readable: show it to the developer as the explanation of what plays and why. If you must hand-edit a line, satie_check validates it for free. Never invent audio file paths: only clipPaths returned by satie_realize exist.
